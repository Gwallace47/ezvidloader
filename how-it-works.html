How it works

From a multi-hour broadcast to a 30-second clip, step by step.

1. Watching for GTA 6 streams

Using Twitch's public API, ezvidloader checks which streams are live in the Grand Theft Auto VI category. When a broadcast ends, the archived stream becomes available to process. Streamers on the opt-out list are skipped.

2. Finding highlight moments

Only the audio of the archived stream is downloaded. The tool measures loudness over time and marks moments where the streamer or the game suddenly gets much louder than usual, which is a good sign that something happened. Those moments are transcribed on the operator's own computer.

3. Choosing the best clips

Short transcripts of the candidate moments are sent to an AI model (Anthropic's Claude), which picks the moments that work best as standalone short videos, sets the exact start and end, and writes a title and a hook. Weak moments are dropped instead of posted.

4. Editing

Only the chosen part of the video is fetched. It's reframed to vertical 9:16 with a blurred background, word-by-word captions are added, and the streamer's channel (twitch.tv/name) is shown at the top for the whole clip.

5. Publishing

The operator logs in once with TikTok Login Kit (scope user.info.basic) so the tool can read the account's display name and allowed privacy settings. Clips are then posted to the operator's own TikTok account with the Content Posting API (scope video.publish), with a caption that credits the streamer and relevant hashtags. YouTube Shorts are uploaded the same way with the YouTube Data API.

What it doesn't do

  • It doesn't post to anyone else's account and has no public sign-up.
  • It doesn't re-upload full streams, only short highlights.
  • It doesn't collect data about viewers.
  • It doesn't keep stream audio: it's deleted after processing.
